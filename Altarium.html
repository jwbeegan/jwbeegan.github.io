Play Altarium Here

Altarium is an abstract strategy game themed around placing items on an altar. Your goal is to gain enough devotion every round to meet the round goal. Every turn you can place 2 of 3 items from your stock onto the board, and the various effects inscribed in your doctrine dictate how many points you will get. The player picks 1 of 3 new items or rules between every round. If a rule is not inscribed at the start of a round, it is lost. The player must survive 20 rounds to win.

One of the main design goals of Altarium was a focus on emergence. This was not clear early on in the jam, but became a focus as we refined the design through many prototypes. The rules the player can inscribe were made to be very simple. By combining the rules, the player is able to make combos that more effectively increase their devotion. We also wanted to inject risky decisions to add weight to the players actions, which is why the player can only select 2 of 3 items at a time, and must discard rules that are not inscribed at the start of the round. In that way, I think the game is a success. I think it's fun to find the synergies in the rules, and make risky decisions with what you keep. There is definitely room for improvement though, some of the rules are definitely too powerful.

However, I think we made a major stumble with our UI and tutorial implementation. While Altarium is not the most complex game, some players were left confused by our tutorial. I wanted to try and strip the tutorial down to its bare essentials. This was a reaction to Germstones, who's tutorial is much too long. However, I think we missed the mark and left too many players confused about the rules and their goals. This is compounded by the devotion UI. During development we floated the idea of giving the player rewards for going 2x above the devotion requirement in a round. This idea did not make the final cut, but the devotion UI still resets the bar when you fill it. I think this lead a lot of players to assume that the bar was being emptied, when in reality the round goal had been met. I think a UI pass to remove all the extraneous indicators would have gone a long way to making the game more clear for those who were confused. I think the players that did stick with the game really enjoyed it, reflected in both feedback and our final judge rating.

Overall I am happy with the game despite it's flaws. I think Altarium's design is as good as Germstones, but where Germstones UI and game feel is superb, Altariums's is a bit lacking. Next time I hope to make another strong original design while nailing the UI/UX and tutorial.
